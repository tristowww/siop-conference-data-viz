Meaning 3 of 4

Why I think the shift matters.

What interests me is the range of questions people are asking about AI. Alongside scoring and prediction, the program includes more discussion of how people use AI at work. That tells me about these conference records, not how the whole field has changed.

Here, I-side means selection and assessment. O-side is teams, leadership, learning, culture, employee experience, and work design.

What stays

Selection and assessment still matter.

AI still shows up in the expected I-side places: scoring, prediction, validation, fairness, cheating, and assessment design.

What changes

More discussion focuses on people at work.

Training, coaching, leadership, culture, accessibility, and work design depend more on judgment, trust, timing, and the messiness of real work.

Why it matters

The design problem changes.

For coaching, leadership and culture, I'd also want to ask whether AI helped people understand or make a decision about their work. Getting a score right is only part of what I'd want to evaluate.

So what 4 of 4

I want to understand what people are using AI for.

In these collected SIOP records, AI discussion includes a wider range of work contexts over time. Selection and assessment remain part of it, alongside more visible discussion of training, coaching, leadership, culture, accessibility and work design.

What changed More AI discussion across more topics

More sessions mention AI, and org-side topics become easier to see in the program mix.

Why it matters The design problem changes

For these uses, I'd want to consider judgment, trust, learning and the situation people are working in.

How to read it Keep the source in mind

The source records differ across years. These charts describe the collected sessions; they don't establish adoption across the field or workplaces.
