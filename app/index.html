Meaning 4 of 5

Plain-English read

Why I think the shift matters.

I think the read is simple: this does not prove the whole field changed, but it does show AI moving from "Can we score or predict this?" into "How do people actually use this at work?"

Plainly: I-side is selection and assessment. O-side is teams, leadership, learning, culture, employee experience, and work design.

What stays

Selection and assessment still matter.

AI still shows up in the expected I-side places: scoring, prediction, validation, fairness, cheating, and assessment design.

What changes

The newer story is more human-contextual.

Training, coaching, leadership, culture, accessibility, and work design depend more on judgment, trust, timing, and the messiness of real work.

Why it matters

The design problem changes.

Once AI moves into coaching, leadership, and culture, the question is less "Did the model get the score right?" and more "Did this help people make better sense of work?"

So what 5 of 5

So what

The sharper story is where AI is showing up.

Across the public SIOP program data, AI appears to be moving beyond a mostly assessment-and-methods conversation into a broader set of people-centered applications. Selection did not disappear. The newer pattern is that training, coaching, leadership, culture, accessibility, and work design are becoming easier to see in the AI conversation.

What changed More volume, broader use cases

More sessions mention AI, and org-side topics become easier to see in the program mix.

Why it matters The design problem changes

O-side AI is less about one score and more about judgment, trust, learning, and context.

How to read it A conference pattern, not a causal claim

The charts describe conference sessions and examples, not definitive field-wide adoption.
